Food delivery intelligence: from messy data to decisions

How it was built

Source

Two public Zomato datasets. Downloaded from pinned commits and checksum-verified.

Profile

Counted what was wrong before touching anything: placeholder NaNs, impossible values, spreadsheet-mangled times.

Clean with a ledger

Every rule logs rows affected and the action taken. Flag before delete; only exact duplicates are dropped.

Model in SQL

SQLite staging and marts, tested to reconcile to the fact table.

Detect

Robust outlier scores, control charts, gap detection, missingness analysis.

Explain

Interpretable regression with a time-based split and confidence intervals.

Publish

Aggregates only. Four dashboards and a build-your-own tool, no server.

What this data can and cannot tell you

Be sceptical, as the project itself is. The delivery file has real-looking mess (spreadsheet-corrupted times, GPS zeros) but clean, near-deterministic patterns: all 22 cities average within a minute of each other, categories are evenly split, and GPS offsets take 12 values. It reads like generated data. The restaurant file shows none of those signs, though its ratings are only as good as the review counts behind them. Findings demonstrate method, and the Data quality page shows the evidence for every caveat.
Limits. Delivery data covers 11 Feb to 6 Apr 2022, with a ten-day gap and different cities in February than in March and April. Only Bangalore's 36 reporting days are a like-for-like series. Neither source states a licence, so raw data is not redistributed here and the dashboards publish aggregates only.
